# ADHD Study AI — two-minute passion-project walkthrough

*[Start on the landing page, then move naturally through the dashboard, planner, and a guided study session.]*

“This is ADHD Study AI, a passion project that came directly from my experience as a student with ADHD.

I usually knew I needed to study. The hard part was everything before I could begin: figuring out what mattered, breaking it into manageable steps, and choosing where to start. Planners assume that thinking is already done, while a chatbot does not understand my real classes, deadlines, or workload. I wanted to build the missing layer between them.

The dashboard reflects that idea. Instead of showing everything, it brings forward today’s work, the nearest deadline, and one useful next action.

From the planner, a student can upload a real syllabus. The app extracts the course, assignments, exams, and dates, but the AI never silently writes to the schedule. The student reviews source evidence, sets their availability, and previews a capacity-aware plan. If life interrupts it, the catch-up tool protects work they completed, started, pinned, or edited, and its changes can be reviewed and undone.

Starting a task opens a timed study session with the assignment and course material already in context. The tutor guides instead of completing the work. That material can also become saved study guides, editable flashcards, and practice quizzes with immediate explanations.

I built the product with Next.js, React, TypeScript, Supabase, and OpenAI. Underneath it are row-level security, private storage, schema-validated and source-grounded AI output, cost telemetry, accessibility work, and browser, database, and unit testing.

What I am most proud of is that this is not a chatbot with a study-themed interface. It is one connected path from messy coursework to a realistic plan, focused work, and visible progress. It started as something I wished I had, and it now represents how I like to build: understand the problem deeply, care about the unglamorous details, and keep refining until the entire experience feels coherent.”
